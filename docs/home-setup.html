What actually matters: graphics memory

A model runs fast when its whole file fits in the graphics card's own memory (VRAM), with some room left over for your conversation. When the model doesn't fit, part of it spills into regular memory and speed drops sharply. So pick hardware by VRAM first and by speed second.

  • 16 GB of VRAM runs 14B-class models fully on the card. For example, qwen3:14b is about 9 GB.
  • 32 GB runs the 30B-class models that come close to paid chat quality for everyday work. For example, gemma4:31b is about 20 GB.
  • 96 GB to 128 GB runs 70B-class models and larger on a single machine.
  • Unified memory (Apple Silicon, AMD Ryzen AI Max, DGX Spark) is shared between processor and graphics, so most of it counts as graphics memory. Speed then depends on memory bandwidth, which is listed for each pick.

One graphics card for the PC you have

This is the most direct upgrade if you already have a desktop PC with room for a full-size card and a big enough power supply.

Top pick

GeForce RTX 5090: 32 GB GDDR7

The most VRAM on any GeForce card. It runs 30B-class models entirely on the card at full speed, with room for long documents.

Power: 575 W for the card. NVIDIA lists a 1000 W power supply for the whole system. Who it's for: people who want the best single-card local setup. Who should skip it: anyone whose case or power supply can't take it, and anyone who only runs 8B to 14B models.

Big-memory desktops

These keep the model in a large pool of shared memory instead of a graphics card. They fit far bigger models than any single consumer card, and answer more slowly per word than an RTX card on models that fit both.

Compact

NVIDIA DGX Spark: 128 GB unified memory

A small desktop box built for AI, with 128 GB of memory shared between the processor and the graphics side. NVIDIA says it handles models of up to 200 billion parameters, or 405 billion with two linked together. The trade-off is memory bandwidth: 273 GB/s is far lower than a desktop RTX card, so a model that fits on both answers faster on the RTX card. The Spark is for running models too big for any GeForce card.

Storage: 1 TB or 4 TB. Who it's for: people who want very large models in a quiet box on the desk, without building a PC. Who should skip it: anyone mainly running 14B to 30B models, where an RTX card is faster.

Most memory

Apple Mac Studio: up to 512 GB unified memory

The M5 Max model goes up to 128 GB with 614 GB/s of bandwidth (on the 40-core GPU version). The M5 Ultra model starts at 96 GB, goes up to 512 GB and runs at 1.2 TB/s. That's enough to run the largest open models on one quiet desk machine, and more bandwidth than DGX Spark. This guide works on it as written.

Who it's for: people who want the biggest models in silence, or who already work on a Mac. Who should skip it: anyone who wants NVIDIA's software ecosystem, or only runs 14B to 30B models.

The rest of the build

Power supply

Match or beat NVIDIA's listed system power for your card (750 W, 850 W or 1000 W above), and use the power connector the card needs. An underpowered supply is the most common cause of crashes under load.

Who needs it: anyone adding an RTX 5080 or 5090 to an older PC. Who can skip it: DGX Spark buyers, since it ships with its own power.

Frequently asked questions

Is the RTX 5090 worth it over the RTX 5080 for local AI?

For local AI, yes, if you want 30B-class models. The 5090 has 32 GB of VRAM against 16 GB on the 5080, and memory decides which models fit. If you'll stay at 14B-class models, the 5070 Ti runs the same sizes as the 5080 for less.

DGX Spark or an RTX 5090?

An RTX 5090 is faster on any model that fits in its 32 GB. DGX Spark's 128 GB lets you run much larger models that a 5090 can't hold at all, but more slowly per word. Pick by the biggest model you actually plan to run.

Can I use two graphics cards together?

Ollama can split a model across more than one NVIDIA card, but you need a motherboard, case and power supply built for it. For most people a single card with more memory is simpler.

Mac Studio or an NVIDIA PC?

A Mac Studio holds much bigger models (up to 512 GB of memory) and stays quiet. An RTX 5090 PC answers faster on models that fit in its 32 GB. If the models you want are bigger than 32 GB, the Mac Studio is the simpler machine.

Can I use an AMD graphics card?

Yes, on cards Ollama supports through AMD's ROCm software. It takes more setup than NVIDIA, especially on Windows, so check Ollama's supported list first.
