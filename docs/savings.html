What you save

If you run local AI on a computer you already own, you save the whole subscription. If you buy hardware, here is how long it takes to pay for itself.

The short answer: if you run local AI on a computer you already own, you save the full subscription from the first month. If you buy hardware for it, you save money only after the hardware pays for itself, and that can take years if you are replacing a standard $20-a-month plan.

The $0 route

Every tool in this guide is free: Ollama, the model weights, Docker Desktop for personal use, and Open WebUI. If your computer has 16 GB of RAM, you can run a competent model today. Your only marginal cost is setup time and a few cents of daily electricity.

If you buy hardware

What you give up

Local AI is a trade, not a free lunch. Know these before you cancel anything.

  • The biggest cloud models. Paid plans run huge models on data center hardware. A local model on a 16 GB laptop handles everyday writing, summarizing and editing well, but it will not match a top cloud model on hard math or large code changes. A 32 GB graphics card or a 128 GB AI desktop closes much of that gap. See the hardware page.
  • Your chats on your phone. Chats live on your computer. Reaching them away from home means leaving that computer on and setting up a private network such as Tailscale.
  • Disk space and a short wait. Each model takes a few gigabytes to tens of gigabytes of disk, and the first message after a break waits a few seconds while the model loads.
  • Web search out of the box. Local models answer from what they learned in training and from your files. Open WebUI can add web search, but you have to switch it on in its admin settings.

Frequently asked questions

How much electricity does running local AI consume?

For typical desktop usage (a few hours of daily interaction), an Apple Silicon Mac adds around $1 to $2 per month to your electric bill. A desktop PC with a dedicated NVIDIA GPU running several hours daily adds approximately $3 to $7 per month depending on local utility rates.

Is it financially smart to buy a new computer just to avoid a $20 subscription?

No. Spending $1,000 to $2,000 on new hardware takes 4 to 8 years to break even against a standard $20-per-month plan. New hardware only makes financial sense if you are upgrading for general computing needs or replacing heavy $100 to $200 per month plans.

Are there any hidden recurring fees with local AI?

No. Ollama, Open WebUI, and open-weight models are completely free to use. There are no monthly maintenance charges or recurring license fees.

How does pay-as-you-go API usage compare to subscriptions?

If you occasionally need frontier cloud models, paying per token through an API provider often costs $1 to $5 per month for light use, compared to a flat $20 subscription.
