Move your AI workspace off subscriptions and onto free local AI

This is the whole process for replacing a paid AI plan such as Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini with a workspace that runs on your own computer. You install a free model runner, download a model that fits your memory, pick a free workspace app, connect the two, and move your project over.

Works on Mac, Windows and Linux · About 1 to 2 hours, mostly downloads · Software cost: $0 · Last checked November 6, 2026

What replaces what

A paid AI plan bundles four things together: the model, the chat app, your projects, and sometimes an agent that does tasks for you. On a local setup each one is a separate free tool, and this guide installs them in order.

What you pay for nowFree local replacementStep
The AI model (Claude, GPT, Gemini)An open model such as Gemma, Qwen or Llama, run by Ollama3 and 4
The chat app in your browserOpen WebUI or LibreChat, which run in Docker5 to 7
Projects with instructions and filesA model preset with a system prompt and a knowledge base inside the workspace app8
An agent that runs tasks (like Claude Code)Hermes Agent, a free open-source agent that can use your local model5 and 7

Set your expectations first. A local model is not Claude or GPT. How fast and how capable it is depends on your computer's memory and the model you pick. Expect it to handle everyday writing, summarizing and questions about your own files well, and expect it to fall short on hard reasoning and very long inputs. Step 9 shows how to test this before you cancel anything.

Part 1

The process, step by step

Do the steps in order, because each one depends on the one before it. Every step ends with a check, so you know it worked before you move on.

1Save your current setup

Copy your project instructions and files out by hand before you change anything. The parts that make a project behave the way it does are its instructions and its files, and no AI service exports a whole project as one package you can import somewhere else.

  1. Make a folder called ai-migration, with one subfolder for each project you want to keep.
  2. Open each project and copy its custom instructions into a text file named instructions.md in that project's subfolder.
  3. Download each file attached to the project into a files subfolder, keeping the original names.
  4. Copy anything you want to keep from the service's saved memory or personalization settings into memory.md.
  5. As a backup, request the full account export. In Claude it is under Settings, then Privacy, then Export data. In ChatGPT it is under Settings, then Data controls, then Export data. For Gemini, use Google Takeout. The download link is emailed to you and expires, so save the file right away.

You can skip old chats. Most of them are not worth moving, and the instructions and files are what recreate the project.

Check: every project subfolder has an instructions.md and a files folder with every document you want the new setup to know about.

2Check your computer

Find out how much memory your computer has, because it decides which models you can run. The model has to fit in memory alongside everything else you have open.

Mac

Open the Apple menu and choose About This Mac. Note the chip (M1 to M4 or newer, or Intel) and the memory. On Apple Silicon the graphics share that same memory, so the memory number is the one that matters. Ollama's docs ask for macOS 14 Sonoma or newer. Intel Macs work but run models on the processor only, which is much slower.

Windows

Open Settings, then System, then About, and note the installed RAM. Then open Task Manager, choose Performance, and click GPU to see your graphics card and its dedicated memory. A recent NVIDIA card with 8 GB or more of its own memory makes local models much faster. Without one, models still run on the processor, just slower.

Linux

Run free -h to see your memory. If you have an NVIDIA card, nvidia-smi shows its model and memory. As on Windows, the card's own memory decides how big a model runs fast.

Free up disk space too. Keep at least 20 GB free for the apps and your first model, and plan for several more gigabytes for each extra model.

Check: you know your memory (RAM, and graphics memory on a PC) and you have 20 GB or more free.

3Install Ollama

Ollama is the free program that downloads AI models and runs them on your computer. Every workspace app in this guide talks to your model through Ollama, so it goes in first. Get it from ollama.com/download.

Mac

  1. Download the Mac version, open the file, and drag Ollama into Applications.
  2. Open Ollama from Applications. If it asks to add its command to your system, allow it.
  3. On Apple Silicon it uses the graphics automatically. There are no drivers to install.

Windows

  1. Download the Windows installer and run it.
  2. Ollama starts in the background and shows an icon near the clock.
  3. If you have an NVIDIA card, keep its driver up to date so Ollama can use it.

Linux

Run the official install script:

curl -fsSL https://ollama.com/install.sh | sh

Then open a terminal (Terminal on Mac, PowerShell on Windows) and check it:

ollama --version

Check: the terminal prints a version number. If it says the command is not found, close the terminal, open it again and retry.

4Download a model sized to your memory

Pick a model whose download is no bigger than about half your memory. A model that is too big either will not load or answers painfully slowly. On a PC with a graphics card, size it to the card's memory for full speed. Bigger versions of the same model are smarter but slower, so start in your row and move up or down after you try it.

Your memoryAim for a download ofModels to start with
8 GBup to about 4 GBqwen3:4b (2.5 GB)
16 GBup to about 8 GBqwen3:8b (5.2 GB) or gemma4:12b (about 8 GB)
32 GBup to about 16 GBqwen3:14b (9.3 GB) or gemma4:26b (16 to 19 GB, a tight fit)
48 GB or more20 GB and upgemma4:31b (about 20 GB) or qwen3:30b (19 GB)

Sizes are from the Ollama library pages, checked November 6, 2026. They are starting suggestions, not benchmarks. Models change often, so check ollama.com/library for what is current.

Download and try the model in your terminal. Replace the name with the one from your row:

ollama run qwen3:8b

The first run downloads the model, which can take several minutes. Then a chat opens in the terminal. Ask it something, and type /bye to leave. These two commands show what you have downloaded and what is loaded right now:

ollama list
ollama ps

Check: the model answers a question at a speed you can live with. If replies crawl, pick a smaller model.

5Choose your workspace app

The workspace app is the window you work in every day, and it replaces the Claude or ChatGPT website. All of these are free and all of them can use the model you just downloaded. Pick one to start. You can add another later, since they all share the same Ollama models.

Start here

Open WebUI

The closest thing to a Claude project. A chat window in your browser with model presets, a system prompt per preset, and knowledge bases you upload files to.

Needs: Docker. Best for: most people.

Free

LibreChat

A ChatGPT-style chat window that can switch between your local model and any paid service you keep, in one place.

Needs: Docker and Git. Best for: people mixing local and cloud models.

Free

Hermes Agent

An open-source agent from Nous Research that does tasks rather than just chatting. It runs in the terminal and can also connect to apps like Telegram or Discord.

Needs: no Docker, but a larger model. Best for: replacing a coding or task agent.

Prefer a regular desktop app over a browser window? LM Studio and AnythingLLM are free desktop apps that skip Docker. This guide covers the three above because they give you a full project workspace, but those two are good options if Docker gives you trouble.

Check: you have picked one app. If you are not sure, pick Open WebUI.

6Install Docker (for Open WebUI or LibreChat)

Docker runs the workspace app in a sealed box, so it installs the same way on every computer and does not touch the rest of your system. Skip this step if you chose Hermes Agent only. Docker Desktop is free for personal use and small businesses. Check Docker's pricing page if you use it for a larger company.

Mac

Download Docker Desktop for Mac, choosing the Apple Silicon or Intel version to match your chip. Drag it into Applications, open it, and wait until it says Docker is running.

Windows

Download Docker Desktop for Windows and run the installer. Keep the WSL 2 option selected when it asks. Restart if prompted, then open Docker Desktop and wait until it says Docker is running.

Linux

Install Docker Engine using the instructions for your distribution, which include the Compose plugin that LibreChat needs.

docker --version

Check: the command prints a version, and Docker Desktop (on Mac and Windows) shows it is running. Ollama must also still be running.

7Install the workspace and connect it to your model

Follow the part for the app you picked. In each case the app finds your model by talking to Ollama at port 11434 on your own computer. From inside Docker that address is host.docker.internal:11434, and from outside Docker it is localhost:11434.

Option A: Open WebUI

Run this one command in your terminal. It downloads Open WebUI, starts it, and points it at Ollama on your computer. This is the method Open WebUI's own docs recommend.

docker run -d -p 3000:8080 --add-host=host.docker.internal:host-gateway -v open-webui:/app/backend/data --name open-webui --restart always ghcr.io/open-webui/open-webui:main
  1. Wait a minute or two, then open http://localhost:3000 in your browser.
  2. Create an account. It lives only on your computer, not online. The first account you create is the admin.
  3. Start a new chat and choose your model from the menu at the top.

If the model menu is empty, open Admin Panel, then Settings, then Connections, and set the Ollama address to http://host.docker.internal:11434.

Linux: Ollama only listens to your own machine by default, so the container may not reach it. The simplest fix is to run Open WebUI on the host network instead, then open http://localhost:8080:

docker run -d --network=host -v open-webui:/app/backend/data -e OLLAMA_BASE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:11434 --name open-webui --restart always ghcr.io/open-webui/open-webui:main

Check: you can chat with your model in the browser.

Option B: LibreChat

LibreChat installs from its code repository, so you also need Git. Run these in your terminal, one at a time. On Windows, use copy .env.example .env for the third line.

git clone https://github.com/LibreChat-AI/LibreChat.git
cd LibreChat
cp .env.example .env
cp docker-compose.override.yml.example docker-compose.override.yml

Now tell LibreChat about Ollama. Create a file named librechat.yaml in the LibreChat folder with this in it. The API key can be any word, because Ollama ignores it.

version: 1.2.1
endpoints:
  custom:
    - name: "Ollama"
      apiKey: "ollama"
      baseURL: "http://host.docker.internal:11434/v1/"
      models:
        default: ["qwen3:8b"]
        fetch: true
      titleConvo: true
      titleModel: "current_model"

Open docker-compose.override.yml and make sure the lines that mount librechat.yaml are not commented out (remove the # in front of them). Then start it:

docker compose up -d
  1. The first start takes a few minutes. Then open http://localhost:3080.
  2. Click Sign up and create your account. The first account is the admin.
  3. Pick Ollama from the endpoint menu, then your model.

Edited the YAML file later? Restart with docker compose down and then docker compose up -d.

Check: Ollama appears in the endpoint menu and your model answers.

Option C: Hermes Agent

Hermes needs a bigger model and a bigger working memory (its "context") than plain chat, because an agent reads tool results as it works. Its docs suggest gemma4:31b, which needs around 24 GB of memory or more. Install it with the command for your system:

Mac

curl -fsSL https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/install.sh | bash
source ~/.zshrc

Windows (PowerShell)

iex (irm https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/install.ps1)

Close and reopen PowerShell afterwards.

Linux

curl -fsSL https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/install.sh | bash
source ~/.bashrc

Then give your model a bigger context. Ollama's default context is small, and Hermes' docs say agent work needs at least 64,000 tokens. Make a plain text file named Modelfile with these two lines:

FROM gemma4:31b
PARAMETER num_ctx 64000

In the same folder, build a copy of the model with that setting:

ollama create gemma4-64k -f Modelfile

Now connect Hermes to it. Run hermes setup, choose Custom Endpoint, and enter:

  • Base URL: http://localhost:11434/v1
  • API key: leave it empty
  • Model: gemma4-64k

Then start it with hermes.

Check: Hermes answers using your local model. A bigger context uses more memory, so if it becomes very slow, lower num_ctx or use a smaller model.

8Rebuild your project as one workspace

Recreate each old project as a preset: your local model plus your instructions plus your files, saved under the project's name. Here is how in Open WebUI, which has the closest match to a Claude project.

  1. Add your files. Click your profile icon, then Workspace, then Knowledge, then create a new knowledge base. Name it after your project and upload everything from that project's files folder.
  2. Add your instructions. Go to Workspace, then Models, and create a new model. Give it your project's name, choose your downloaded model as the base, and paste instructions.md into the System Prompt box.
  3. Attach the knowledge. In the same screen, attach the knowledge base from the first step, and save.
  4. Add your memory. Paste anything from memory.md that should always apply onto the end of the system prompt.

Your project now appears in the model menu of every new chat. In LibreChat, the matching feature is Agents, where you set instructions and attach files. In Hermes, paste your instructions into its config or first message, and point it at your project folder.

Read your old instructions through as you paste them, and cut lines that mention features the new setup does not have, such as artifacts or connected apps.

Check: start a chat with your project preset and ask it to restate the project brief in its own words. Then ask something only one of your files can answer.

9Test it before you cancel

Run your five most common real tasks on the new setup before you cancel your subscription. A local setup covers everyday work well and falls short on the hardest tasks, and only your own tasks can tell you which side you are on.

TaskTypical local result
Drafting, rewriting, summarizingUsually good enough for everyday use
Questions about your own filesWorks once the knowledge base is set up; quality depends on the model
Brainstorming and outlinesUsually good
Hard multi-step reasoning or mathOften noticeably weaker than the top paid models
Large codebases or very long documentsLimited by how much text the model can hold at once
SpeedSet by your computer, not a server

What you gain: no monthly fee, no usage limits, and your files never leave your computer. Once set up, it works without the internet.

What you give up: the extras around the model, such as artifacts, synced projects across devices, and connected apps. Some have local versions, but you set them up yourself.

The middle path: keep a free account with a cloud AI for the few tasks where the local model disappoints, and do everything else locally. You drop the paid plan but keep a fallback.

Check: your everyday tasks work. Now you can cancel the paid plan with confidence.

Troubleshooting

Most problems come from one of three things: Ollama is not running, Docker is not running, or the model is too big for your memory.

ProblemWhat to do
ollama command not foundClose and reopen the terminal. If it persists, open the Ollama app again and allow it to add its command.
Workspace loads but shows no modelsMake sure Ollama is running. In Open WebUI, set the Ollama address under Admin Panel, Settings, Connections to http://host.docker.internal:11434. On Linux, use the host-network command in Step 7.
The localhost page will not openOpen Docker Desktop and check that the container is running. Give it a minute or two after starting.
Port already in useFor Open WebUI, change -p 3000:8080 to -p 3001:8080 and open localhost:3001.
Replies are very slow, or the model will not loadSwitch to a smaller model, or close other heavy apps.
The model forgets things in long chats or long filesIts context is too small. Raise num_ctx as in Option C, if your memory allows.
Disk is filling upRun ollama list, then remove models you don't use with ollama rm and the model name.

Updating Open WebUI later: run docker pull ghcr.io/open-webui/open-webui:main, remove the old container with docker rm -f open-webui, and run the Step 7 command again. Your chats and presets live in the open-webui volume, so they stay.

Part 2

What you save by leaving AI subscriptions: the break-even math

The short answer: if you run local AI on a computer you already own, you save the full subscription from the first month. If you buy hardware for it, you save money only after the hardware pays for itself, and that can take years if you are replacing a $20 plan.

The $0 route

Everything in the guide above is free: Ollama, the models, Docker Desktop for personal use, and every workspace app. If your current computer has 16 GB of memory or more, you can run a useful model today, and your only costs are your setup time and a little electricity.

If you buy hardware

Months to break even is the hardware cost divided by the monthly plan you cancel. The hardware amounts below are examples, not product quotes, and they leave out electricity and your time.

ScenarioHardwarePlan replacedBreak even
Use the computer you own$0$20 a monthRight away
Modest upgrade$600$20 a month30 months
Modest upgrade$600$100 a month6 months
AI desktop$2,000$200 a month10 months
AI desktop$2,000$20 a month100 months

The last row is the honest one. Buying an expensive machine to replace a $20 plan does not pay off. Hardware makes sense for heavy users on the $100 and $200 plans, or for people who want privacy and no limits more than they want the savings.

Work out your own break-even

Turn on JavaScript to use the calculator.

Electricity depends on your power rates and how many hours a day you use it. The $3 default is an example; check your own bill.

What you give up

A local model is not Claude or GPT, so the saving only counts if the local model does the work you need. That is why the guide ends with a test before you cancel. If it handles your everyday tasks, keep a free cloud account for the hard ones and pocket the difference.
