8Rebuild your project as one workspace

In Claude or ChatGPT, a "Project" or "Custom GPT" bundles three elements: a base model, custom instructions, and reference documents. Rebuilding this inside Open WebUI takes about five minutes per project.

  1. Upload your reference files:
    • In Open WebUI, click Workspace in the left sidebar, then click Knowledge.
    • Click the + button to create a new Knowledge collection. Name it after your project (for example, Client Q4 Project).
    • Drag and drop all files from your ai-migration/[project]/files/ folder into the collection.
    • Wait for the processing bar to finish indexing each file.
  2. Create your custom model preset:
    • In the sidebar, click Workspace, then click Models.
    • Click the + button to create a custom model.
    • Set the Name to your project name.
    • Under Base Model, choose the local model you downloaded (e.g. qwen3:8b).
    • Open your instructions.md file, copy its text, and paste it into the System Prompt field.
    • If you have global memory notes from memory.md, paste them at the bottom of the System Prompt under an ### Additional Context header.
  3. Attach the Knowledge collection:
    • Scroll down to the Knowledge section on the same screen.
    • Select the collection you created in step 1.
    • Click Save & Create at the bottom.

What normal looks like: click New Chat in the sidebar. In the model selector at the top of the chat, your custom project model appears with its custom name. When selected, it automatically applies your instructions and has access to your reference files.

If this goes wrong

  • File upload fails or hangs: very large PDFs (over 50 MB) can choke the local embedding parser. Convert large PDFs to clean plain text or Markdown files first, then re-upload.
  • Model ignores the knowledge documents: in Open WebUI, you can force document retrieval by typing # followed by the knowledge base name in your chat prompt.
  • Old instructions cause errors: review your pasted instructions and remove lines referencing proprietary cloud features like Claude Artifacts, Code Interpreter, or OpenAI DALL-E image generation.
  • A scanned PDF uploads but the model says it's empty: scanned pages are pictures with no text in them. Upload a version where you can select the words, or a .txt or .md copy.
  • The model ignores parts of a long instruction prompt: smaller local models follow short numbered rules far better than long paragraphs. Cut your instructions down to the 5 to 8 rules that matter most.

Check: start a new chat with your project preset. Ask it: "Summarize the primary purpose of this project and list three specific facts from the attached reference documents." It should cite your files accurately.

9Test it before you cancel

Before canceling your paid subscription, test your local setup against your five most common daily tasks. A local setup handles writing, editing, and private document questions well, but can struggle with large-scale programming or difficult mathematical logic.

TaskWhat to expect locallyHow to evaluate
Drafting, editing, rewritingFast and fluentCheck tone, formatting, and adherence to your style guidelines.
Searching and querying your filesAccurate when documents are clearAsk specific questions that can only be answered by your reference files.
Brainstorming & outliningComparable to commercial modelsCheck creativity, organization, and variety of ideas.
Hard coding and complex debuggingGood for functions, weaker on large codebasesRun tests on generated code; verify edge cases manually.
Complex math & multi-step logicNoticeably weaker than top cloud modelsDouble-check calculations and multi-step reasoning chains.

Trade-offs to weigh:

  • What you gain: $0 monthly subscription fee, complete document privacy, no message caps or peak-hour rate limits, and full offline operation.
  • What you lose: automatic cloud synchronization across your phone and tablet, proprietary artifact sandboxes, and the raw reasoning depth of the largest frontier models.
  • The hybrid approach: many people downgrade to the free tiers of Claude or ChatGPT. They use their local setup for 90% of daily private work, and fall back to free cloud chats only when they hit a tough math or coding problem.

If this goes wrong

  • The model forgets the start of a long document or conversation: raise the context length. In Open WebUI, open Chat Controls (the sliders icon, top right), then Advanced Params, and set Context Length to 8192 or 16384. Longer context uses more memory.

Check: your daily workflow passes testing. You can now visit your provider's account page and safely cancel your paid subscription.

ollama command not foundTerminal path not loadedRestart your terminal. On Mac, open Ollama from Applications and allow command installation.

How to update Open WebUI in the future:

docker pull ghcr.io/open-webui/open-webui:main
docker rm -f open-webui
docker run -d -p 3000:8080 --add-host=host.docker.internal:host-gateway -v open-webui:/app/backend/data --name open-webui --restart always ghcr.io/open-webui/open-webui:main

Your chats, files, and custom model presets are saved inside the open-webui Docker volume, so they are preserved when updating container images.

Frequently asked questions

Can local AI search and read my PDF, Word, and text files?

Yes. Open WebUI features a built-in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system. When you upload PDFs, text, Markdown, or spreadsheets to a Knowledge collection, it chunks and embeds them locally to pull relevant facts into chat.

What is context length and why does it matter for my files?

Context length is the total amount of text a model can hold in its active memory during a conversation. If you upload a book or have a very long conversation, smaller context windows may cause the model to lose track of earlier details.

When should I keep a free cloud account instead of cancelling fully?

Keep a free tier account on Claude or ChatGPT for rare, highly demanding tasks: solving hard programming bugs, verifying legal/financial language, or generating complex charts that local models struggle with.

How do I back up my Open WebUI chats and knowledge bases?

All data is saved inside the Docker volume named open-webui. You can also export individual chats, presets, and documents directly from the Open WebUI Settings and Workspace screens.
